# Executive evidence review at scale

Across the current 37 accepted pilot contacts, ten came from official team cards with direct LinkedIn links, two from the earlier Luna verifier using fetched official pages and fetched LinkedIn pages, four from earlier spot checks, twenty from the later source-by-source review below, and one from the new citation verifier. The 18 covered companies have a mix of these paths; they were not all reviewed in the same way.

## What the 20 manual decisions required

The 20 reviewed pilot additions used six evidence patterns. These counts describe the **sources used**, not an estimate that the new code can reproduce every record today.

| Evidence pattern | Contacts | Example | Programmatic treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Official company team/story page names the role; indexed personal profile identifies the company | 8 | Trexo CEO and CTO | Python checks exact company domain, name/role proximity, exact `/in/` URL, and current company in the profile excerpt. |
| Official team page names the role; the person's own LinkedIn post links them to the company | 3 | Swift CTO | Needs a future self-post rule with author-URL and recency checks; currently stays pending unless an indexed personal profile independently matches. |
| University, industry body, or company-issued wire release names the role; profile supports identity | 3 | Spring Loaded CEO | Python can classify source and extract a dated statement; Luna can interpret unstructured context. Current citation-only acceptance deliberately excludes this class until publisher and recency rules are tested. |
| Official company news item names the role; profile supports identity | 3 | HDAX CEO and COO | Python can parse publication date and company-domain identity. Old announcements must not establish a *current* role without newer corroboration. |
| Company LinkedIn post names the role; personal profile supports identity | 2 | Flutter Care CEO | Needs a rule establishing the company page belongs to the workbook entity and the post is recent. Not yet auto-accepted. |
| Personal profile is the principal role source | 1 | Densitas CEO | Keep pending until an independent current role source is found. A profile alone is too weak for the stated false-positive tolerance. |

For each person, the manual check first confirmed the workbook company and official site, then a current executive title from a company-controlled or attributable source, then the *exact* LinkedIn profile rather than a similar name. It also checked for departures, closures, acquisitions, and conflicting roles. Emails were recorded only when an individual business address was published in a source; none were inferred from a domain pattern.

## Division of work

Python handles deterministic work: stable workbook row IDs, site discovery and redirects, page crawling, name/title extraction from team cards, exact LinkedIn URL normalization, source-domain matching, search-result citation capture, recency and conflict flags, duplicate removal, three-person role ordering, per-call cost limits, caching, and workbook export. A 403 or LinkedIn 999 is recorded as an access failure. It does not turn into a negative employment finding.

Luna handles narrow interpretation and search: suggest the official entity when the workbook lacks a website, find executive names and role pages, search for missing exact personal profiles, and flag a possible departure or same-name collision. It returns structured candidates and source URLs. Its statement alone cannot accept a contact or an email. The Python verifier checks source excerpts returned by the search provider against the proposed person, role, company, and exact personal URL. A failed check leaves the record pending.

The implemented citation fallback accepts only a current official team/about page paired with an indexed *personal* LinkedIn profile that explicitly shows the current company. This is deliberately narrower than the manual review. The provider's excerpts are retained in `outputs/canada_executive_research/luna_cited_evidence.json` so a reviewer can audit a match even when direct access is blocked.

## Pilot result and rollout gate

An isolated Swift Medical replay with no pre-accepted contacts recovered CEO Dwayne Sansone, CTO Stratos Davlos, and Chief Strategy Officer Mark Douglas from a cached role search plus one new targeted profile search. The new search cost about **$0.008**. Applied to the live pilot ledger, the same mechanism filled Swift's third slot with Chief People Officer Cerys Cook; her official role, exact personal URL and current-company excerpt passed the Python checks. The pilot now has **37 contacts across 18 companies**: five companies have three, thirteen have one or two, and seven remain empty.

This is a smoke test, not a measured precision or recall estimate. Before a 1,503-company run, replay the verifier on a fixed labeled sample with both true matches and known traps (Optina's former CEO, Radialis same-name profiles, inactive Swiftsure, acquired Janpix, and ambiguous Kyva). Measure accepted, rejected, and unverified records separately, and review every automatic acceptance in that sample. Then run a bounded batch with a call limit and a spend cap, inspect the evidence registry, and expand only if false matches remain at zero in the reviewed sample. The exact-profile citation path will miss valid people when search indexing omits their company; those should stay blank or enter a separate, explicitly labeled review queue.

The current OpenRouter web plugin returns structured Luna output and extractive citations. OpenRouter has deprecated that plugin in favor of its server web-search tool. In this pilot the server tool returned prose despite the JSON schema request, so the code retains the working plugin while migration is tested. This is a dependency to resolve before unattended full-scale operation, along with source freshness rules for dated posts and wire releases. See the [OpenRouter web-search documentation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/server-tools/web-search).

Inputs contain only workbook company details; historical contacts, profiles and prior model leads are withheld. Three companies can run concurrently in separate directories.

## Company identity and role verification replay

An offline replay of the same 64 claims investigated the 33 company identity/activity failures and 11 role-evidence failures. It made **zero new API calls** and incurred **zero additional spend**. Results are isolated in `outputs/canada_executive_research/broad_25_gate_v5_2026-09-28`.

General fixes:

- A fetched request to the workbook/working homepage may verify its observed redirect when the destination homepage names the same company. The verified destination becomes the official host for subsequent role/profile checks. A model-suggested unrelated page cannot supply this redirect chain.
- An unresolved workbook alias flag can be resolved only for `shared_domain_different_names` records with one matching workbook domain and names differing solely by spacing, trailing legal suffixes or the specified medical/device descriptors. The fetched homepage must still name the company. Missing metadata, unrelated names and multiple domains remain unresolved. The source worksheet row, alias values and workbook hash are retained; the workbook flag itself is not changed.
- Homepage identity checks inspect all title, main-heading and footer elements instead of stopping after eight tags.
- Official `/meet-the-team`, `/meet-our-team` and `/our-team` pages qualify as team pages under the existing recency rules.
- Compound executive titles such as President & CEO and intervening professional credentials are recognized. Person/role matching rejects a neighboring person's name between the proposed person and title, and explicit former-role labels. It remains conservative about ambiguous title placement.

Selected contacts increased from **7 to 12**; pending contacts decreased from **57 to 52**. Company identity/activity failures decreased from **33 to 19**, and role failures from **11 to 9**. Nineteen original individual gate failures were cleared: five candidates then passed all checks, while fourteen hit another check. Some newly verified identities exposed role failures, so the combined identity/role category fell from 44 to 28 rather than by nineteen.

Ten of the twelve selected profiles match the original 37. Of the other two, one was already historically accepted but outside its company's top-three selection, and one is a new candidate. The historical 37-contact ledger remains unchanged. None of the known trap companies gained an acceptance. This is a programmatic replay, not an independent manual precision audit.

The remaining identity/role diagnostics are seven candidates at inactive companies; one with uncertain company activity; seven with unavailable homepages; four with homepage-name mismatches; two relying only on personal profiles for role evidence; two needing newer role corroboration; three with unqualified external/previous-domain role sources; and two sources that do not pair the person with the claimed role. Detailed transitions are in `BOTTLENECK_REPORT.md` and `bottleneck_diagnostics.json`.

The focused suite passes **51 tests**, ledger validation has no issues, and gate replay is repeatable without new requests. Gate source hashes and decision history are retained so changes to verification code trigger an explicit reassessment. The next useful work is targeted corroboration for unavailable or renamed company sites and examination of the ten profile-identity failures now exposed. Inactive entities and unsupported roles remain pending.
